# sbmlsim-core 0.29.0

The forward sensitivities carry the changes and the steady state of a request, so the jacobian of a fit of doses at times, or of conditions which start from a steady state, is the derivative of its residuals and no longer a difference of them.

## Features

- **Sensitivities across the changes of a request** (#104). A change is applied to the state and the parameters moved along every sensitivity as well, and the difference of the two afterwards over the step is the sensitivity after it: a value set to a number has no derivative after the change, one a number is added to (a dose) keeps it, a form `constant + Σ factor * term` takes the derivatives of its terms, and the conversions of the change (compartments and the concentrations they keep, amounts and concentrations, the assignment rules) are carried as the change applies them. A parameter which a change sets to a number no longer depends on the target after it.
- **Sensitivities through a steady state** (#104). The steady state before the start and after the end is an integration over growing horizons, and the sensitivities are integrated with it: the derivatives of the state the integration ends in.

The HCTZ model of pkdb_models, every condition of which starts from the drug-free steady state of its fluid PD, gets the Fisher information of its fits from the sensitivities: its standard errors agree with the ones of the central differences of sbmlsim to 3.5e-4.

## Fixes

- The sensitivities at a change are read from the integrator before it also where the segment has no output time, and a segment which is started for a change alone starts its sensitivities.

## Unchanged

- The jacobian of a step of a fit and of a point of a profile stays the difference quotient by default (`JacobianChoice::Auto`): on the profiles of the HCTZ PK fit the sensitivities took 162 s against 85 s for the differences.
- What a run of sensitivities refuses: the events of a model, a discontinuity of its math, an algebraic rule, a delay and the iterative linear solver.
