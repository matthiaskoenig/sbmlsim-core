# sbmlsim-core 0.28.0

The figures get their last features: colour bars which say what they show, legends which keep out of the data, text styles and an interaction layer for a page. A figure which a 0.27.0 spec describes renders as before, with the three exceptions listed under Behaviour changes.

## Features

- **Honest colour bars** (#100). A `ColorScale` takes `start` and `end`, the part of its ramp it uses, so a scale which shows a subrange does not paint the whole ramp. A `ColorBar` takes `ticks` and `tick_labels` at positions of its own and `categorical`, which draws one swatch per category instead of a ramp, and a plot lists its bars in `Plot.colorbars`. Python: `color_scale` takes `start` and `end`.
- **Inside legends which avoid the ink of the data** (#100). A legend inside a plot is placed where it covers the least of the data, and when no place covers less than `INSIDE_LIMIT` (0.05) of its area with ink it moves outside, right of its plot. The limit was tried on 114 typical PK plots.
- **Text styles** (#100). Font weights, `Fonts.tick_y` for the font of the ticks of the y axis, and text over several lines with `\n`.
- **Marks** (#100). Hollow markers, outlines of bars, `Axis.tick_marks_visible` to draw the labels of an axis without its tick marks, and `Plot.legend_swatches` for the shape of the swatches of a legend.
- **Legends of rows** (#100). The row legends follow `legend_order`.
- **Picks along curves** (#100). `pick` finds a point along the segments of a curve and not only at its data points.
- **The interaction layer** (#100). In JavaScript `attachFigure` and `hydrateFigures` make the figures of a page respond to the pointer, and in Python `figure_script` writes the script of a page which holds figures.
- **The text measure in Python** (#101). `text_width` measures a text as the renderer lays it out, so a caller sizes a figure to its labels without drawing it.

## Behaviour changes for 0.27.0 specs

Every golden of 0.27.0 renders byte-identically but for these three cases:

- a bar whose style gives `line` now gets an outline in it
- `\n` in a title, an axis label, a legend label, a text element or a colour bar label now splits it into lines; tick labels and the text of the cells of a heatmap stay one line as before
- an inside legend over dense ink, also over a heatmap, now moves outside, right of its plot
