# sbmlsim-core 0.41.0

A model compiles together with its RODAS4 integrator into one WebAssembly module, the batched kernel (`kernel/1`), which advances many instances of the model per call (#151). A PEtab fit stops at the noise of its simulations: its tolerances default to the relative tolerance of the integrator (#162, fixes #161). The live demo runs 0.40.0 and fits its PEtab problem in the page (#160).

## The batched kernel (#151)

```bash
sbmlsim-core compile model.xml --kernel -o kernel-dir   # kernel.wasm and kernel.json
```

```js
const { wasm, description } = compiler.compileKernel(sbmlText);
const kernel = await instantiateKernel({ wasm, mathlib, memory });
kernel.init(n, parameters, state);
const failed = kernel.advance(n, state, parameters, control, counts, context, scratch);
kernel.assign(n, state, parameters, control);
```

- **One module.** A model of ordinary differential equations and its integrator compile into
  one WebAssembly module, which advances any number of instances of the model per call, the
  cells of a tissue in a page, say, where a call into `engine.wasm` per cell and step would
  cost more than the step itself.
- **Compilation.** `sbmlsim-core compile model.xml --kernel -o DIR` writes `kernel.wasm` and
  `kernel.json`; the same compilation is `sbmlsim_compiler::compile_kernel` in Rust
  (`sbmlsim_codegen::emit_kernel` underneath), `compile_kernel` of `compiler.wasm` and
  `compiler.compileKernel(sbml)` of `js/compile.mjs`, all byte for byte the same.
  `sbmlsim-abi` carries the contract: `KernelDescription`, `KERNEL_CONTRACT = "kernel/1"` and
  the names of the exports.
- **Module.** `js/kernel.mjs` (`sbmlsim-core/kernel`) instantiates a kernel with mathlib and
  the `WebAssembly.Memory` of its caller, `instantiateKernel({ wasm, mathlib, memory })`, after
  checking its imports and its exports.
- **Exports.** `sbmlsim_kernel_init(n, parameters, state)`,
  `sbmlsim_kernel_advance(n, state, parameters, control, counts, context, scratch)` and
  `sbmlsim_kernel_assign(n, state, parameters, control)`, over arrays of the caller with the
  values of the points one point after the other. `control` holds `t`, `h`, `done` and
  `failed` of a point, `counts` its accepted steps and rejected step attempts, `context` the
  `t1`, `rtol`, `atol`, `h_min` and `attempts` of the QFunction. All three give -1 and change
  nothing for invalid arguments (a negative `n`, a misaligned pointer, an array past the end of
  the memory, a bad context).
- **Nothing of its own.** The kernel imports only `env.memory` and functions of mathlib, has no
  data segment and no global and never grows the memory: the caller sizes it for its points.
- **`init`** takes a parameter which is NaN as not given: it gets its SBML value and its initial
  assignment, every other parameter is fixed, as a pre-initialization value of a simulation is.
  With the parameters of an initialized engine `init` gives the states of the engine.
- **`assign`** evaluates the assignment rules of every point at its time and its states and
  writes their targets into its parameters, as `model_assign` does in the engine, so an output
  which is the target of a rule of a state or of the time (the necrosis trigger of the cell
  model, say) has its value after an advance. `kernel.json` lists these parameters as
  `assigned_ids`, in the order they are evaluated.
- **The integrator of the QFunction.** RODAS4 with the step control, the first step (HINIT) and
  the dense LU decomposition of the QFunction target of the C target, operation for operation.
  On the cell model of `testdata/models` (200 parameter sets, 24 intervals of 60 min) a point
  takes exactly the steps of the QFunction, its states within about `1e-14` relatively. The
  math functions are those of mathlib, so the results are not bitwise those of the C library.
  A point's steps depend on nothing but the point, so one call for all points and one call per
  point give the same bits.
- **Refusals.** The kernel refuses what the QFunction refuses, naming the feature: events,
  delays, algebraic rules, a model without states or without an analytic Jacobian.
- **Tests.** `crates/codegen/tests/kernel.rs` runs the kernel in wasmtime against the
  QFunction (steps and states), the engine (`init`), the C target (`assign`) and itself in
  blocks of any size, and checks the imports, exports and sections of the module;
  `js/test/kernel.test.mjs` holds `compileKernel` to the command line byte for byte and runs a
  kernel in Node.

The kernel advances one point after the other; SIMD is not used yet.

See [Compiling a model](https://matthiaskoenig.github.io/sbmlsim-core/compiling/#a-batched-kernel)
and [JavaScript](https://matthiaskoenig.github.io/sbmlsim-core/javascript/#the-batched-kernel).

## The tolerances of a PEtab fit (#162, fixes #161)

- **Defaults.** Where the settings of a PEtab fit give no tolerance, `gradient_tolerance`,
  `step_tolerance` and `cost_tolerance` (the `gtol`, `xtol` and `ftol` of
  `scipy.optimize.least_squares`) are the largest relative tolerance of the integrator of the
  problem, at least `1e-8`, as sbmlsim's `default_tolerance` takes them. A simulation is exact
  only to that tolerance, so a fit stops at the noise of its cost instead of chasing it.
- **Everywhere.** `Plan::fit_tolerance` and `Plan::fit_settings` of `sbmlsim-petab` set them,
  for `Executor::fit` (the command line `sbmlsim-core petab --fit` and `sbmlsim_core.petab` in
  python) and for `petab_fit_open` of `compiler.wasm` (`problem.fit()` of `js/petab.mjs`), so
  the page still fits as the command line does. A tolerance the caller gives wins
  (`gradientTolerance`, `stepTolerance`, `costTolerance` in JavaScript, the settings in Rust
  and python).
- **Effect.** On the problem of the demo and the 49 cases of the PEtab test suite whose fit the
  engine supports, the statuses go from 21 `NumericalNoProgress`, 6 `SolverFailed` and 23
  `SolverConverged` to 42 `RelativeCostTolerance`, 3 `RelativeParamTolerance` and 5
  `SolverConverged`, with 439 evaluations instead of 1048 (the demo 5 instead of 25). The
  optima are the earlier ones, their objectives within `1e-9` relative, but for the three
  cases whose objective has no minimum (v2 0015 and 0021, v1 0015: an estimated noise with a
  lower bound of 0 runs to 0), which stop at their step tolerance on the way.

See [PEtab](https://matthiaskoenig.github.io/sbmlsim-core/petab/#the-fit).

## Behaviour changes

- A PEtab fit whose settings give no `gradient_tolerance`, `step_tolerance` or
  `cost_tolerance` takes the largest relative tolerance of the integrator, at least `1e-8`,
  instead of the defaults of the optimizer: it ends at `RelativeCostTolerance` or
  `RelativeParamTolerance` where it ended in `NumericalNoProgress` or `SolverFailed`, after
  fewer evaluations and at the same optimum. This holds for `sbmlsim-core petab --fit`,
  `sbmlsim_core.petab` and `problem.fit()` of `js/petab.mjs` (#162).
