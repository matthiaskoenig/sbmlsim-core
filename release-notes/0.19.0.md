# sbmlsim-core 0.19.0

Two corrections of the engine a caller reaches from python: the residual of a curve is the one its caller computes, bitwise, and the native engine is the engine of every call and no longer of the simulations alone.

## The residual of a measurement is divided by its standard deviation (#64)

The residuals of a fit were computed as `(simulated - measured) * (weight / noise)`: the weight over the standard deviation was folded into a scale once, per point, and every residual multiplied by it. A reciprocal rounds, so such a residual is not the one its caller computes with a division. `0.9 / 2.5` is `0.36` where `0.9 * (1.0 / 2.5)` is `0.36000000000000004`, one unit in the last place apart.

The residual is now `(simulated - measured) / noise * weight`, the division its caller writes, and the weight is applied after it, so a curve of the weight 1 is the division alone and the cost of a fit of the engine is the cost of its caller to the last bit. Nothing of the arithmetic changed for a standard deviation which is a power of two, which is why the fits of the test suites agreed before: their measurements carry the deviation 1.

The jacobian keeps its scale, the derivative of a residual being linear in it.

For [sbmlsim](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim) this is what makes a fit of a likelihood a fit of the engine: a likelihood divides the difference of a measurement by the scale of its noise, which is a number of the data and not a power of two, and of the 31 cases of the PEtab v2 test suite 27 are now a fit of the engine whose residuals are the ones of the problem bitwise.

## The native engine reads the initial values and resolves a plan (#65)

The native engine (`sbmlsim-engine` compiled for the machine with the model in wasmtime, about twice as fast as `engine.wasm`) simulated the points of a batch and the evaluations of a fit, while two calls of a caller which has it still instantiated `engine.wasm`:

- `LoadedModel::initial_selections` for a model with an algebraic rule or a delay, whose initial values the engine computes rather than the direct evaluation of the host. Every load of a model in python reads them.
- `LoadedModel::prepare`, which validated a reusable request and resolved its plan.

Both use the native engine now, so the initial values a caller reads and the plan its points run come from the engine which runs them, and a build with the feature `native` instantiates `engine.wasm` nowhere. The results of the two engines are not bitwise each other's (the C library of the host in the step sizes of CVODE), so reading the initial values of a DAE model in the engine of its simulations is the consistent one of the two.

`engine.wasm` remains the engine of the browser, which the JavaScript host loads, and of a build without the feature.
