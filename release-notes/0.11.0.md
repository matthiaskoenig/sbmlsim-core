# Release notes for sbmlsim-core 0.11.0

The engine integrates delay differential equations, large stiff models can ask for a matrix-free linear solver, and a batch of the bindings is no longer one request with many points: it is the batch problem a parameter fit is, which the python bindings now run in one fan out over their pool.

## New

- **Delay differential equations** ([#38](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim-core/pull/38), closes [#31](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim-core/issues/31)). A model which uses `delay(expression, duration)` compiles and simulates with a history of its states, its parameters, its assignment rules and its initialization, and a historical algebraic unknown is projected at the time it is queried. Nested, variable and reversing delays, historical concentrations, the changes of an event and of a request, and a history across the phases of a steady state are covered. The engine selects a separate integrator for such a model once: it records the dense polynomials CVODE and IDA accept, locates the discontinuities the history propagates and iterates overlapping steps instead of capping every step at the shortest delay. An ordinary ODE keeps a history of size zero and its own callbacks, and every one of the 1,701 models which were supported before compiles to the same bytes of WebAssembly and the same description. All 53 prepared cases of the delay feature pass in wasmtime and natively, and the semantic suite of the SBML Test Suite passes 1,754 of its 1,789 cases; the 35 which are left need fast reactions. [Delay differential equations](https://matthiaskoenig.github.io/sbmlsim-core/delays/) is the page of the feature, and `bench/dde-performance/` holds the frozen measurement of what it costs an ordinary ODE, which the merge accepted: ten of 48 workloads measured a slowdown, the largest of them 3.90 % with a simultaneous interval of 2.40 % to 5.44 %.

- **Preconditioned GMRES for large stiff models** ([#37](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim-core/pull/37), refs [#33](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim-core/issues/33)). `settings.linear_solver = "gmres"` runs CVODE with BDF and Newton on matrix-free Jacobian-vector products with an ILU(0) left preconditioner, which the `jacobian` of the request builds analytically or from coloured differences. It is opt-in and stays so: on the 919-state liver of a machine perfusion it is 1.29 to 1.32 times the native time of KLU and 1.42 to 1.43 times its time in wasmtime, so the automatic choice of a solver still takes KLU, and at `relative_tolerance` 1e-12 every sampled state agrees with a KLU reference at 1e-13 within `1e-12 + 1e-7 |reference|`. A request which asks for GMRES with Adams or with IDA is refused and says what to set instead. The evaluation, with its commands and its raw measurements, is `bench/GMRES.md`.

- **A batch is the work of a fit** ([#41](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim-core/pull/41), [#42](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim-core/pull/42), for [sbmlsim#556](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim/issues/556)). The pool of a batch is the crate `sbmlsim-batch`: a rayon pool with one engine instance per thread, made at the first point of the thread and reused by every point after it, and `Workers::map` runs the whole work of a point on the thread of the point, so a caller whose point is more than a simulation (the residuals of an objective, the rows of a table) does that work there as well. The python bindings keep the GIL, the objects of python and the check of the signals and nothing else.

    On top of it, `BatchExecutor.simulate_plans` takes pairs of a prepared request and the values of its varying targets, so one evaluation of an objective (a simulation per condition at the same parameters) and the whole finite difference Jacobian of one are a single fan out instead of a call per condition, which was a serial run with a pool attached. Measured on a fit of hydrochlorothiazide with `scripts/objective_timing.py`, one batch of plans is 4.6 to 10.6 times a serial loop and 1.9 to 5.4 times a process pool of the same width, and 4.6 times a call per condition on a plain evaluation, which that call cannot spread over the threads at all (0.96x). `bench/OPTIMIZATION.md` has the tables, what a request of `simulation/1` costs against a prepared one (nothing worth having: 1.03 to 1.05 times) and where the other half of the threads goes.

## Fixed

A review of the three features of this release and of the algebraic rules of 0.10.0 along speed, usability and robustness ([#39](https://github.com/matthiaskoenig/sbmlsim-core/pull/39)):

- **The retained delay history grew with the horizon.** A run kept the dense polynomial of every accepted step for the whole simulation, so a chain of 20 Mackey-Glass equations with a delay of 2 reached 1,638 MB at a horizon of 6,400 and a chain of 100 failed at t = 1,675 for want of memory, although neither reads further back than two time units. The compiler now publishes `max_delay`, the longest chain of nested durations, when every one of them is a finite nonnegative constant, and the engine drops the records and the jumps below twice that bound and rebuilds its arena without them. The same model peaks at 16.7 MB, flat across every horizon, and runs in 2.65 s instead of 3.51 s; against a control whose lag is a non-constant parameter, so that nothing is pruned, the trajectory is bitwise identical. A duration which depends on the state or on the time keeps every step, as before, and says so when it runs out of room. The description of an ODE model is unchanged and omits `max_delay`.

- **Three refusals which named neither the cause nor the remedy**: an algebraic rule whose references are all determined was called overdetermined, both refusals of GMRES read the same sentence, and a change of an entity a rule determines named neither the rule nor which kind it was. Each one now names the entity, the rule or the field of the request which rules the simulation out, and what to set instead.

- **Panics inside the callbacks of IDA.** The Jacobian of IDA and the attachment of GMRES reached for their sparsity pattern and their workspace with `expect`; a native host catches that, `engine.wasm` aborts, so a missing workspace would have trapped the instance instead of reporting an error. The callbacks now report it, and the sparse Jacobian checks that the matrix of IDA holds the entries it is about to write.

The pull request also lists what the review looked at and left alone, with the reason: the code bloat the ordinary ODE workloads measure, the stickiness of an invalid lag inside one step, the prehistory which is recomputed per callback, the delay sites which share a lag and share nothing, and the knobs GMRES has not got.

## Compatibility

The contracts `model/2` and `simulation/1` are unchanged. The private format of the output between a host and the engine is 2, since the buffer of the statistics grew with the counters of the iterative solver: a host and the runtime artifacts it loads (`sbmlsim-core runtime -o DIR`) are upgraded together, and a host of this version does not read an `engine.wasm` of an older one.
