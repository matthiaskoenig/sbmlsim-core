# sbmlsim-core 0.34.0

The chart builder of sbmlsim is a module of the python package, `sbmlsim_core.chart`, so packages below sbmlsim in the dependencies (pkpdutils, pkdb) draw charts without sbmlsim (#126). The renderer draws the fourth round of the ported figures of pkdb_models as matplotlib draws them: legends of the figure above the grid and under a column, fonts and frames of every legend, edges of bars and bins, the drawing order across both y axes, colour bars below a row, fonts per axis, and raster images under the data (#127).

## The chart builder in python

- **`sbmlsim_core.chart`** (#126): `Chart`, `ChartPlot`, `ChartAxis`, `LEGEND_LOCS`, `MARKERS`, the colours of matplotlib and the thinning of dense curves, moved from `sbmlsim.plot.chart` of sbmlsim 0.48.0, which re-exports them. The package still depends on numpy only; `docs/chart.md` describes it.

### Quantities and data arrays without pint and xarray

pint and xarray are read by duck typing and never imported at module level (a test checks that drawing a chart leaves both out of `sys.modules`):

- a quantity is anything with `to(unit)`, `magnitude` and `units`. It is converted by its own registry with the unit of the axis as text (`quantity.to("hr")`), so the units of the registry it was made with are known, those sbmlsim and a model define included. An axis without a unit takes `str(quantity.units)`. A quantity of another dimension raises the `DimensionalityError` of its own registry with the axis in its message, as before; any other failure is a `ValueError` naming the axis.
- a data array is anything with `values`, `dims` and `attrs`, its unit `attrs["units"]`. On an axis of its unit, or on an axis without a unit, which it gives its unit, it is taken as it is, without pint. One of another unit is converted with the application registry of pint (`pint.get_application_registry()`, imported by name only then); without pint that raises a `ValueError` which says so. A unit of a data array is read only when it is converted: on an axis without a unit, a unit pint does not know is shown as given (before, it raised).
- an axis unit is kept as text and shown as given when it is no unit, as before.

`short_unit` formats the labels of the units without pint: a small parser of pint's expressions (products, quotients, `**`/`^`, brackets, juxtaposition, `1/...`) over the SI prefixes and the units of pharmacokinetics and physiology, written as `f"{unit:~P}"` of pint (sorted by the names of pint, `⋅`, superscripts, cancelling units, the offset rule of `degC`). It equals pint's form on 273 units, the units of pkdb_models, pkpdsim and sbmlsim and their long forms; the test `test_short_unit_is_the_one_of_pint` compares it with pint when pint is installed. A text it does not read (a factor, a unit of a model) is shown as given, as pint's failure was before.

## Features

- **Images**: element `image` of `png` (base64 of a PNG, at most 64 Mpx, its header checked), `extent` (`[x0, x1, y0, y1]` in the values of the axes), `origin` (`upper`, `lower`) and `interpolation` (`auto`, `smooth`, `nearest`), matplotlib's `imshow(image, extent=..., origin=...)`: stretched over its extent (an extent running the other way mirrors it), the axes sticking to its edges, at the opacity of its style, under the other elements (`z` -1); `auto` draws an image enlarged three times or more with square pixels and smooths it else, as matplotlib's default. Embedded in the SVG as a `data:image/png;base64` URI and drawn by resvg in the PNG (its raster images enabled, PNG of a data URI only, never a file). `sbmlsim_core.image_png(pixels)` gives the `png` of an RGB(A) array of bytes or of shares of 1.
- **Legends of the figure above the grid**: `position: "top"` of a legend of the figure (`fig.legend(loc="outside upper center")`), below the title, the figure grown by it; **under or over a column**: `column` of a legend `top` or `bottom`, centred on the panels of that column, the legends of different columns side by side.
- **Room for a legend anchored beyond its panel**: a `legend_anchor` (or `anchor` of a further legend) below 0 or above 1 reserves the room of the part of the legend beyond the panel on that side, and the figure grows when it would still reach out of it, instead of the legend being cut off.
- **Fonts and frames of every legend**: `size`, `weight` and `frame` of the legends of the figure and of the further legends of a plot, `legend_size`, `legend_weight`, `legend_family` and `legend_frame` of a plot (`frameon=False`: no frame and no background).
- **Drawing order**: `z` of an element (`zorder`), ordering the elements of the left and the right y axis together; a plot which needs no other order draws the same bytes.
- **Edges of bars and bins**: `edge_color`, `edge_width` of bars and histograms (`edgecolor`, `linewidth`, e.g. white edges); the swatch of bars and of a histogram draws the edge, so a white bin with a black edge shows in its legend.
- **Colour bars**: placement `bottom` (a horizontal bar below the panels of a row and their axes, `location="bottom"`), `tick_side` (`top`, `bottom`) of a horizontal bar (`ticklocation`), `fonts.colorbar_label_weight` and `fonts.colorbar_tick`.
- **Fonts of an axis**: `tick_weight` and `label_size` of an axis.
- **Boxes**: `median_opacity` (seaborn's opaque median in a translucent box).
- **Chart builder**: `sbmlsim_core.chart` takes every new field, written into the spec only when given: `plot.image(image, extent, origin=, interpolation=, opacity=, z=)` (PNG bytes, base64 or an RGB(A) array), `z=` of every element method, `edge_color`, `edge_width` of `bar` and `histogram`, `median_opacity` of `box`, `placement="bottom"` and `tick_side` of `colorbar`, `legend_size`, `legend_weight`, `legend_family`, `legend_frame` of `Chart.plot`, `size`, `weight`, `frame` of further legends and of the legends of the figure, `position="top"` and `column` of the legends of the figure, `ChartAxis(tick_weight=, label_size=)` and the fonts `colorbar_tick` and `colorbar_label_weight`; a paragraph and a figure in `docs/chart.md`.

## Behaviour changes of the renderer

- The opacity of an arrow with a `body` is one opacity of the whole arrow, as matplotlib draws the alpha of its patch; its edge over its fill was darker before.
- A contour corner is above a level when its value is greater than it (contourpy's `z > level`): a level at the least value of the data draws the edge of that region (zero lines), one at the largest draws nothing, as matplotlib; checked against the lines of matplotlib 3.11.

## Unchanged

- A spec of 0.33 parses as before; every new field is optional.
