# sbmlsim-core 0.26.0

A batch of python uses the cores it runs on, CVODE and IDA repeat less of their work per step, the basis of a polynomial chaos expansion moves into the engine, and a result can carry what the integrator estimated of its own error.

## Features

- **The basis of a polynomial chaos expansion.** `chaos_expansion`, `chaos_design_matrix` and `chaos_predict` of the python bindings, and the crate `sbmlsim-chaos` behind them, are the parts of the expansion of `sbmlsim.sensitivity.chaos` that are regular and cheap per entry (#86).
  - `chaos_expansion` gives the multi-indices of a truncated basis (the hyperbolic truncation `q` and a cap on the interaction) in the order of sbmlsim.
  - `chaos_design_matrix` gives the shifted Legendre polynomials normalized to one at the points of a sample, bitwise the ones of sbmlsim.
  - `chaos_predict` gives the values of an expansion at any points, its surrogate, without the design matrix.

  A term skips the inputs it does not read, and the points are spread over the threads of the process. For HCTZ (22 inputs, degree 4, 782 terms) the design matrix of 2048 points takes 3.4 ms on one thread instead of the 106 ms of numpy, and the surrogate of 100k points and 101 elements takes 0.19 s on eight threads instead of 7.7 s. The least squares of the fit stay with sbmlsim, whose QR is the one of LAPACK.

- **The local error estimates of the integrator.** `"local_errors": true` of a request makes every row of the result carry two vectors over the states of the model, both of the last step the integrator took before the row (#89):
  - `errors`: the estimate of the local error of that step (`CVodeGetEstLocalErrors`, `IDAGetEstLocalErrors` for a DAE model)
  - `weights`: the weights of its error test, `1 / (rtol |y| + atol)` of the state the step started from

  The weighted root mean square norm of the two is at most 1 for a step CVODE accepted, so it is the share of its tolerance the step used, which is what a verification of the numerical error reads next to the statistics of 0.25.0. A row which no step precedes is `NaN`. The result carries `local_errors: {states, errors, weights}`, and python hands it over as `out.local_errors` with arrays of the shape `(rows, states)`. The native engine answers it. `engine.wasm`, whose output has no room for it, refuses the request, as it refuses the forward sensitivities.

## Performance

- **A batch of python on many cores.** A batch of `simulate_many` on eight pinned P-cores reached 72 to 80 % of eight threads, where the same batch in Rust reaches 93 to 95 %. The loss was in the bindings (#63):
  - The requests were read one after the other with the GIL before the pool started.
  - The pool ran in slices whose threads waited for the slowest one.

  A batch now reads its requests in ranges while its pool simulates the ones it read before, the threads meet only after the last point, and a request is read without tracking the path of its fields, which is read again only for the message of a request which is none. Short points of HCTZ on eight threads went from 70k to 75k points/s up to 88k to 89k (89 to 95 %), and eight python threads with an executor of one thread each from 63k to 81k. Interrupts are checked every 20 ms while the pool simulates.

  A request which is none is no longer refused before anything runs: the points before it may have run, and the batch stops there with the `ValueError` which names it and gives no result.

- **Less work around CVODE and IDA.** The engine repeats less of what stays the same between the steps and the (re)initializations of a simulation, with the same solver, the same steps and the same numbers (#92):
  - an unchanged setting and the identifiers of the states of IDA are kept, where the limits of a step, the initial step, the stop time and the reset of the roots still apply at every restart
  - a dense matrix is cleared once, and a sparse matrix keeps its structure and the positions of its diagonal over its updates and copies, updating its values only
  - the parameters of a large model stay in the native memory of the solver while a run integrates, invalidated where a change or a sensitivity sets them
  - up to 32 inputs loaded often and never written are cached
  - the storage of the consistent initial values and of the projection of IDA is reused

  The benchmark models of the native engine take 2 to 8 % less CPU time per simulation and synthetic DAEs 7 to 19 %, with the outputs and the counts of the solver of all 18 cases unchanged.

  A model compiled without the analytic derivatives of its right hand side by its parameters does less to compile and to initialize: `compile_sbml(sbml, parameter_jacobian=False)` in python, `--no-parameter-jacobian` of the command line, `CompileOptions::parameter_jacobian` in Rust and `compiler.compile(sbml, { parameterJacobian: false })` in JavaScript, whose `compiler.wasm` exports `compile_with_options` for it. The results of a simulation are the same, and the forward sensitivities of such a model take the difference quotient for the derivatives. The default keeps them.
