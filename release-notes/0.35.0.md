# sbmlsim-core 0.35.0

The thinning of the chart builder runs in Rust, the new crate `sbmlsim-thin`, and keeps the same points about 20 times faster (#129). A fit of `fit/1` takes a curve over the points of a dimension of a scan, which interpolates between their conditions as `numpy.interp` does, and a parameter pinned by a condition, so sbmlsim fits a scan in the engine (#130, follow-up of sbmlsim#624).

## The thinning of the chart builder in Rust

- **`sbmlsim-thin`** (#129, `crates/thin`): the core of `kept_points` of `sbmlsim_core.chart.thinning`, Ramer-Douglas-Peucker, exposed as `sbmlsim_core._core.thin_kept_points(x, y, tolerance, keep=None)` of the extension. `kept_points` keeps its signature and calls it.
- **The same kept points, bit for bit**: a segment is split at its farthest point, the first one on a tie, the distance by the same operations in the same order with the `hypot` of the C library which numpy calls; the order of the splits does not change the result. The farthest point is found on the squares of the distances, and only the points within a relative 1e-9 of the largest square get their `hypot`; a segment whose squares cannot be trusted (not a number, an overflow, subnormal) has every distance computed.
- **Speed**: on the 2602 lines (6.4M points) of the 3 figures of `DoseDependencyExperiment` of the HCTZ model of pkdb_models over its draws, `kept_points` takes 0.19 to 0.25 s instead of 4.4 to 6.1 s (about 20x), the thinning of the elements as a whole 0.34 to 0.48 s instead of 3.8 to 4.0 s. The extent of an axis and the extremes of a column are taken without copies of the values.
- `python/tests/test_chart_thinning_reference.py` keeps the implementation on numpy as the reference and compares the kept points on 21 kinds of line (gaps, jumps, logarithmic axes, flat segments, repeated points, ties, subnormal and overflowing coordinates) over seeds and tolerances; `crates/thin` checks that the screening finds the farthest point of every distance on random segments from subnormal to overflowing scales.

## The points of a scan in a fit

A host fits a scan as one condition per point (sbmlsim#624). Two additive fields of `fit/1` (#130) cover what is no curve of one condition; `docs/fitting.md` describes them under "The points of a scan".

- **`dimension` of a curve**: `{conditions, values, x}`, the conditions of the points of a dimension of a scan, their values (finite, strictly increasing) and the x of every measurement. A measurement between two points is compared with `slope * (x - x0) + y0` between what the curve reads in their two conditions, the arithmetic of `numpy.interp` operation for operation, so the residuals are those of a host interpolating with numpy bitwise; one at a point with what the curve reads in the condition of that point. What a curve reads in a condition is what a curve of that condition alone would read, its column at a row or its reduction; `reductions` gives every point a reduction of its own (the batch of a `pk` with the dose of the point). Its row of the jacobian is the same interpolation of the derivatives of the two points. A curve over a dimension names no `condition` and no `noise_column`.
- **`pinned` of a condition**: `[{parameter, value}]`, a parameter whose target the condition holds at a value of its own, e.g. the point of a scan whose dimension sets the target of a parameter of the fit, which wins over the parameter in sbmlsim. The parameter has no effect on that condition and its column of the jacobian of the curves of the condition is zero; a `derived` value of the condition reads the parameter, not the pin.
- **`sbmlsim_core.FIT_FIELDS`** (`FIELDS` of `sbmlsim-fit`): `["curve.dimension", "condition.pinned"]`, the fields a build reads which an older release would not refuse. A release before 0.35.0 has no such constant, does not deny `pinned` and refuses a curve without a `condition`, so a host reads the constant before it sends either field, as it reads `FIT_REDUCTIONS` before a reduction.
- The Rust API of `sbmlsim-fit` exports `DimensionRequest`, `PinnedRequest`, `Dimension` and `Pinned`.

## Tests

- The benchmark of the renderer (`test_benchmark_time` of `python/tests/test_plot.py`) takes the CPU time of the fastest of its 50 renders (`time.process_time`) instead of the median wall clock, so a loaded machine does not fail it while a regression of the renderer still does, against the same limit of 5 ms (#131).

## Unchanged

- A request of `fit/1` and a spec of `figure/1` of 0.34 parse and give the same results as before; every new field is optional.
- The kept points of the chart builder are those of 0.34.0.
