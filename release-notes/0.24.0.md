# sbmlsim-core 0.24.0

A fit carries its whole objective. A curve of a request of `fit/1` says which likelihood it is and the engine emits the residual rows of the data and of the scale of its noise, and a fit carries the priors of its parameters and their correlation, which the engine evaluates from the values of the parameters alone.

## The likelihood of a curve (#82)

A curve was a weighted least squares and nothing else: one residual per measurement, its deviation divided by the `noise` of the curve. That is not the objective of a fit which estimates the scale of its noise, because the scale moves the normalization of the density as well as the deviation, and a least squares which only divides by it walks the noise to infinity.

- `CurveRequest.likelihood` is the new block of a curve, absent for the weighted least squares of before. Its `distribution` is one of `normal`, `log-normal`, `laplace` and `log-laplace`, and its `noise_column` is a selection of the condition whose value in the row of a measurement is the noise of that measurement, absent for the fixed `noise` of the curve. The number is the standard deviation of a measurement for the two normal kinds and the scale of its distribution for the two Laplace kinds, whose standard deviation is `sqrt(2)` times it.
- A curve of a likelihood has **twice** its measurements as residuals, `[data..., scale...]` per curve in the order of the request, so that half the sum of their squares is the negative log-likelihood of the curve up to a constant per measurement which the host subtracts. The engine never subtracts it: the constant of a measurement of a log kind depends on its own value.
- The rows of the jacobian are the chain rule of both, the sensitivity of the noise column included, so a noise which follows the parameters or the simulation is differentiated and not held. A noise column is one more selection of the condition and no second simulation.
- A measurement of an infinite noise carries no information under a likelihood exactly as it does without one: both of its residuals are `±0.0` and both of its rows of the jacobian are zeros, so it keeps its place in the residual vector. A noise which is zero, negative or `NaN` is a failed measurement.

## The priors of a fit (#82)

A maximum a posteriori fit was no request of `fit/1` at all. The prior rows had to be added to the residual vector by the host, which an optimizer that runs inside the engine never gives it the chance to do, so a fit with priors was a fit the engine could not run.

- `FitRequest.priors` is a prior per parameter, each naming its `parameter` (its position in `parameters`), its `kind`, the two `parameters` of that kind, an `offset`, a `normalizer` and its `lower` and `upper`. The six kinds are `normal`, `log-normal`, `uniform`, `log-uniform`, `laplace` and `log-laplace`. The `normalizer` is the log of the mass of the prior between its bounds, so a truncated prior is one number of the request and no quadrature in the engine.
- `FitRequest.prior_correlation` is one correlated group, a Gaussian copula over the normal scores: the positions of its `parameters`, which of them are `log`, their `location` and `scale`, the `inverse` of the lower Cholesky factor of their correlation, and the `offsets` of its logarithmic members. Its rows are the whitened scores, one per member, and one more per logarithmic member.
- The prior rows come after every curve row, so a host which wants the rows of its data alone takes the ones before them. None of them needs a simulation: a prior is a function of the values of the parameters, and the engine evaluates it without touching the integrator.
- `fisher` of a fit which carries priors is therefore the precision of its **posterior**, the information of the data plus the curvature of the priors, and an interval of its `errors` is a credible interval of the posterior and not a confidence interval of the estimate. For a fit without priors the two are one number.

## The contract stays additive

Every field of both blocks is `#[serde(default)]` and no struct of the request denies unknown fields, so every request a release before this one wrote is read exactly as it was and answers the same numbers, bit for bit. Nothing of `crates/fit` changed in 0.23.0, so this holds against 0.22.0 as well as against 0.23.0.

The same property is a trap in the other direction, and a host has to gate on the version: **a release before 0.24.0 ignores a `likelihood` block, a `priors` list and a `prior_correlation` silently.** It does not refuse them. It answers the row count and the residuals of a weighted least squares without a prior, with no error of any kind, so a host which sends either block to an older engine gets wrong numbers and no sign that anything went wrong. Ask the engine for its version before writing either one.

## Breaking

For a consumer of the Rust crate, `FitError` gained the variants `Prior` and `Correlation` and is not `#[non_exhaustive]`, so an exhaustive match over it no longer compiles. The python bindings are unaffected: `fit_error` has a catch-all arm and both variants map to `ValueError`.
