# sbmlsim-core 0.33.0

The renderer draws the figures of the second wave of pkdb_models as matplotlib draws them: error bars of two sides, contours with inline labels, inset plots, arrows, coloured frames and tick labels, stacked bars, rotated and boxed texts with sub- and superscripts, several legends per panel, the `best` placement and the log axes of matplotlib (#120, #123).

## Features

- **Error bars of two sides**: `xerr`, `yerr` of a curve and `err` of bars are an array or `{"low": [...], "high": [...]}` (matplotlib's errors of shape `(2, n)`), a gap a side which is not drawn (its half of the bar and its cap left out, a one-sided error bar), a side of 0 with its cap at the value as matplotlib. A pick answers `yerr_low` and `yerr_high`.
- **Contours**: element `contour` of `x`, `y`, `values`, `levels`, `filled`, `scale`, `labels`, `label_size`: lines at the levels in the colour of their level with labels inline as matplotlib's `clabel(inline=True)` places them (its block-of-vertices straightness rule, `too_close`, the line broken 5 px beside the label, never upside down), or the bands between the levels (matplotlib's intervals of `contourf`), drawn without antialiasing so bands and cells meet without seams. Marching squares on the cell edges, the lines oriented and started as contourpy's; the axes stick to the ends of the grid. **Power scales**: `gamma` of a colour scale (`PowerNorm`), its bar with the colours evenly along it and the ticks at their shares; `CenteredNorm` is a `center` between symmetric ends. `color_scale(..., gamma=)` in python.
- **Inset plots**: `inset` of a plot, `{"plot", "x", "y", "width", "height"}` in fractions of the host panel (`ax.inset_axes`): own axes, ticks, labels and legend over the host, a white panel, no room of the grid; a legend of the host at `best` goes around it.
- **Arrows**: element `arrow` from (`x_from`, `y_from`) to (`x`, `y`), matplotlib's `annotate` and `FancyArrowPatch`: a line with an `open` (`->`), `filled` (`-|>`) or no head, 2 px shrink at both ends and the tip pulled back by half the line width, or with `body` a filled arrow (`arrowprops` of `width`, `headwidth`, `headlength`, `shrink`); an optional `text` at the tail, the arrow starting at its edge.
- **Frames and coloured tick labels**: `frame` of a plot (`color`, `width`) draws all four sides of the panel instead of the axis lines (the spines of the talinolol and pravastatin scans); `tick_label_color` of an axis, one colour or one per tick or category.
- **Stacks**: `base` of bars and histograms (`bottom`), error bars at the top, the axis sticking to the bases.
- **Markers** `octagon`, `tri_down`, `tri_up`, `tri_left`, `tri_right` (`8`, `1` to `4`), of matplotlib's shapes.
- **Bug, thin rows**: Charton kept a least panel of 100 px, so a grid of rows under about 100 px overlapped its panels and cut off the x label of its last row; the panel of the layout is kept now, and an axis whose tick labels and marks are hidden takes no room for them.
- **Texts**: `rotation`, `baseline` (`baseline`, `center`, `top`, `bottom`), `box` (fill, opacity, edge, edge width, padding, `square` or `round`) and `loc` (matplotlib's `AnchoredText`, without `x`, `y`). The anchors align the box of the rotated text as matplotlib's default `rotation_mode`. Every text draws `_{...}` as a subscript (0.7 of its size, lowered by mathtext's 0.15 em) and `^{...}` as a superscript, measured so; a script of a text of a hanging, middle or central baseline is corrected for the baseline of its smaller size (before, a superscript of a hanging x tick label sat too high).

## Features closer to matplotlib

- `fonts.plot_title_weight`, `fonts.colorbar_label`, `fonts.legend_family`.
- **Legends**: `legends` of a plot (further legends in its panel, each with `groups`, `title`, `loc`, `anchor`, `columns`, `family`, `best` avoiding the legends placed before), `legend_anchor` (`bbox_to_anchor` with `legend_loc`), `legend_tuples` (`HandlerTuple`, the markers of all elements of a group side by side), the swatch of a curve with error bars draws its caps and their width, monospace legends, and `legends` of the figure (`right` of the grid at its top or `bottom`, stacked, taking their groups out of the plot legends).
- **`best` of matplotlib**: the badness of `Legend._find_best_position` (vertices of lines inside the frame, overlapping boxes of bars and texts, lines meeting the frame) replaces the area of ink; the fixtures of the issue (a band, dense curves with error bars) now land where matplotlib puts the legend. Tests rewritten to matplotlib's corners, checked with matplotlib 3.11.
- **Log axes**: the ticks of `LogLocator` (3.11, its stride and `numticks` of the tick space, minor ticks 2 to 9 drawn shorter and thinner, the `AutoLocator` fallback) and the labels of `LogFormatterSciNotation` (decades alone beyond one decade, the minor ticks of 2, 3, 4, 6 above 0.4 decades, all below, including matplotlib's `math.log` quirk at 0.1); the grid at the major ticks. Compared on eight ranges side by side with matplotlib.
- **Colour bars**: `extend` triangles, `rows` (a bar right of its panel spanning rows of the grid), placements `strip` (a strip of the colours along the top of the panel) and `panel` (a bar in place of the panel, a figure of a colour bar alone).
- **Top axis**: `x2` of a plot (`twiny`), its own ticks, labels and limits; the plot title goes above it.
- **Box styles**: `median_color`, `whisker_opacity`.
- **Marks in fractions and unclipped**: `coordinates` (`data`, `axes`) and `clip` (true) of a curve, polygon, text and arrow; unclipped parts are drawn after the panel in a group `sbmlsim-unclipped` with the `data-element` of the element (broken-axis slashes, texts at the edge).
- **Row titles**: `row_titles` of the figure.
- **Seams**: a histogram draws every run of neighbouring bins as one outline (and bar edges only with a line of its style, as matplotlib's `edgecolor`); heatmaps are drawn with `shape-rendering="crispEdges"` as `pcolormesh`.

## Behaviour changes of the renderer

- The ticks and labels of log axes are those of matplotlib's `LogLocator` and `LogFormatterSciNotation`, with minor ticks (#123).
- A legend at `best` takes matplotlib's badness instead of the area of ink, so some legends move to where matplotlib puts them (#123).
- A histogram draws neighbouring bins as one outline, heatmaps without seams (#123).
- A grid of thin rows keeps its layout instead of a least panel of 100 px (#123).

## Unchanged

- A spec of 0.32 parses as before; every new field is optional.
